How the math works

Every AI answer is a run of tokens, and every token is a few hundred millijoules of electricity once you count the chip, its server and the building around it. Set how many tokens a day you want to serve. The model works out how many chips that takes at a given speed and load, what they draw, and how many power plants of one kind you’d need to build to feed them.

chips = tokens a second ÷ (tokens a second per chip × average load)
facility MW = chips × kW per chip × PUE ÷ 1,000
plants = facility MW ÷ MW per plant

What it leaves out

Training. This is serving only. Training runs need their own campuses.

The token mix. Google counts tokens processed, in and out. Reading a prompt is far cheaper than writing an answer, so raise the per-chip speed for traffic that’s mostly long prompts.

The chips Google actually uses. Most of Google’s traffic runs on its own TPUs. The Google scenarios ask what the same traffic would take on NVIDIA hardware.

Spares. Real sites carry backup generation and derate turbines on hot days. Building whole plants, rounded up, is the only margin in here.
